Clinical note

Limitation: this models a nerve membrane. It does not simulate the cardiac action potential, the ECG, or brain water, so it shows the cellular mechanism, not the clinical danger. Not for clinical decisions.

How this works

Resting Vm uses the Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz equation with the pump's 3:2 ratio (r = 1.5, the Mullins–Noda form), with PNa/PK = 0.04. Closing the K⁺ leak drops PK to 0.2, since other K⁺ channels remain.

The pump acts on two timescales. Turning it off sets r = 1, which removes its small direct electrogenic share at once (about 4 mV, rising to about 6 mV as Cl⁻ settles). The larger effect comes slowly, as the gradients run down. Rundown ends at Gibbs–Donnan equilibrium, not at equal concentrations: impermeant intracellular anions (135 mM assumed) hold [K⁺]in near 1.5 × [K⁺]out and Vm near −12 mV, while Na⁺ and Cl⁻ enter and the cell swells. Time is compressed: one simulated minute stands for many minutes to hours in a real cell.

The action potential uses Hodgkin–Huxley voltage-gated Na⁺ and K⁺ channels, with the gating curves anchored to this cell's resting potential, on top of the resting model above. Each stimulus starts from the current resting state, so electrolytes change the AP on their own: hypokalemia hyperpolarizes the cell and raises the stimulus needed; mild hyperkalemia depolarizes it toward threshold; severe hyperkalemia inactivates many Na⁺ channels (h gate), so the AP shrinks. With these squid-axon kinetics the depolarized cell also becomes unstable and fires repeatedly before it reaches full depolarization block, which needs a larger depolarization (closing the K⁺ leak shows it). Plasma Na⁺ barely moves the resting potential but sets ENa, and with it the overshoot. The kinetics are squid-axon rates, so the time course is slower than a mammalian neuron.

Tissue. In nerve, PK is fixed, so hypokalemia makes EK more negative and hyperpolarizes the cell: further from threshold, less excitable. In skeletal and cardiac muscle the resting K⁺ conductance comes largely from inward-rectifier (Kir) channels, whose conductance falls as plasma [K⁺] falls. With less K⁺ conductance the membrane loses its K⁺ dominance and can depolarize paradoxically, inactivating Na⁺ channels: the basis of weakness and paralysis in severe hypokalemia and of arrhythmia risk in the heart. The muscle setting models this as PK ∝ [K⁺]o, a teaching approximation; the real relationship is steeper and differs between heart and skeletal muscle. The action potential uses squid-axon kinetics in both settings.

Threshold is found from the model, not fixed at −55 mV: it is the voltage reached by the smallest 1-ms stimulus that triggers a regenerative upstroke. It shifts with conditions, and when no stimulus up to 80 µA/cm² fires the cell, the page reports it as inexcitable.

Limitations. The model is nerve-focused: Hodgkin–Huxley squid-axon kinetics, one compartment, no Ca²⁺ currents, so it does not reproduce the cardiac action potential (plateau, refractoriness), conduction, the ECG or arrhythmias. The muscle setting changes only the resting K⁺ conductance. Cell volume is an index, not a brain-water model, and ignores the osmolyte adaptation of chronic hyponatremia. Time is compressed. Values are for teaching mechanisms, not for clinical decisions.

Cl⁻ is included with Hodgkin and Katz's squid permeability ratio (PK : PNa : PCl = 1 : 0.04 : 0.45) and redistributes passively over a few seconds. Once settled it carries no net current, so the resting potential is the same as without it, but right after a change in [Na⁺]o or [K⁺]o it buffers Vm. This reproduces Hodgkin and Katz's (1949) finding that lowering external Na⁺ barely moves the resting potential while the overshoot falls roughly as ENa predicts. Max dV/dt is the steepest rate of rise of the upstroke, their most Na⁺-sensitive measure.

Cell volume is an approximate index: intracellular osmoles relative to rest, divided by plasma osmolality (2 × [Na⁺ + K⁺] + 10) relative to normal. Hyponatremia swells cells, which is why it causes cerebral edema; hypernatremia shrinks them.

Advanced: intracellular [K⁺]

While the pump is on it restores [K⁺]in toward 140 mM and [Na⁺]in toward 15 mM, so a change here fades back. Turn the pump off to hold a new value.
